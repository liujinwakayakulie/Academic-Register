# Skill: Mastering Academic Tone

Academic writing is the cornerstone of university assignments, reports, and dissertations. Its primary objective is to ensure the reader understands your perspective through **clear and concise** expression while accurately utilizing professional terminology.

---

## 1. Objectivity

Academic writing must be grounded in evidence rather than personal opinion.

* **Evidence and Citation:** Every argument must be supported by evidence and cited correctly.
* **Neutral Language:** Avoid subjective, judgmental, or emotive language (e.g., *good, bad, great, awful*).
* *Examples:* "This argument has merit because..." or "The policy appears to fail on delivery when compared to..."


* **Third-Person Perspective:** Unless completing a reflective assignment, avoid using "I."
* **Passive Voice:** "The data was recorded."
* **Research-Oriented Subjects:** "This report aims to...", "The findings suggest..."
* **Formal Designations:** "The author", "The researcher", "The student."
* **Impersonal Phrases:** "It has been established...", "It can be seen that..."

---

## 2. Formality

Formal language is typically more concise and professional in nature.

### What to Avoid:

* **Phrasal Verbs/Informal Expressions:** Avoid "look into" (use **investigate**), "huge," or "have a go."
* **Contractions:** Strictly avoid *won't, it'll, isn't*. Use the full forms: *will not, it will, is not*.
* **Slang:** Avoid "kids" (use **children**) or "canny."
* **Colloquialisms:** Avoid "it's a pain" or "get worked up."

### Common Confusion: Its vs. It's

* **It's:** A contraction of *it is* or *it has*. **Avoid this in academic writing.**
* **Its:** A possessive pronoun (like "his" or "her") for genderless nouns. **This is acceptable.**

### Grammatical Accuracy: Comma Splicing

Do not join two independent clauses with only a comma.

* **Incorrect:** "The tree was brown, the tree had green leaves."
* **Correct Revisions:**
1. **Use a conjunction:** ", and"
2. **Use a period:** "The tree was brown. The tree had green leaves."
3. **Use a semicolon:** "The tree was brown; the tree had green leaves." (Only if the clauses are closely related.)

---

## 3. Precision

Language should be direct, accurate, and refined.

* **Eliminate Redundancy:**
* *Redundant:* "The children cooperated **together** during the task."
* *Precise:* "The children cooperated during the task."


* **Eliminate Ambiguity:** Use pronouns like *it, they, this,* and *that* with caution.
* *Ambiguous:* "The project interviewed six small businesswomen." (Does this refer to their physical height or the size of their businesses?)



---

## 4. Tentative Language (Hedging)

In academia, absolute certainties are rare; knowledge evolves with new research. Using cautious language demonstrates intellectual rigor and humility.

### Comparative Examples

| Deterministic (Avoid) | Tentative/Hedging (Use) |
| --- | --- |
| Counselling **helps** to sort out problems. | Counselling **may help** to sort out problems. |
| Rates of infection **differ** between regions. | Rates of infection **appear to differ** between regions. |
| Climate change **is responsible** for this. | **It is possible that** climate change is responsible... |

### Common Hedging Vocabulary:

* **Adverbs:** *probably, often, frequently*
* **Modal Verbs:** *might, could, may*
* **Verbs:** *seems, appears to, suggests*
* **Phrases:** * "It could be argued that..."
* "The evidence suggests that..."
* "It is apparent that..."
* "It is unlikely that..."



---

> **Final Tip:** Before submitting, scan your work for "Hedging." Ensure you have used words like *may, might, seems,* or phrases like *"It is possible that"* to qualify your claims.
